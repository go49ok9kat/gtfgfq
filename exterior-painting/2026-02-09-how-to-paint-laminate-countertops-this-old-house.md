# How To Paint Laminate Countertops - This Old House

Painting laminate countertops is an affordable and creative way to refresh your kitchen or bathroom. This do-it-yourself (DIY) project can transform outdated or worn surfaces into stylish, modern focal points. With the right tools, techniques, and a bit of patience, you can achieve professional-looking results that will breathe new life into your space. We’ll walk you through the process of painting laminate countertops, from preparation to finishing touches, to give you all the information needed for a successful makeover. Laminate countertops are a popular choice for many homeowners because they’re affordable and versatile. What Are Laminate Countertops?

Laminate countertops are made from layers of plastic that are bonded to particleboard or kraft paper to create a solid countertop surface. They’re designed to mimic the look of more expensive materials like granite or wood, offering a wide range of colors and patterns. Laminate is known for its durability and easy maintenance, making it a practical choice for busy households.

Not all laminate is created equal. According to This Old House Magazine, the surfacing is made differently depending on how it’s used. High-pressure laminate (HPL) sheets, backed by layers of kraft paper, come in two grades: thick, for horizontal surfaces like counters, tables, and desktops; and thin, for vertical walls, panels, and cabinet doors. Both kinds are usually glued to particleboard. There’s also low-pressure laminate (LPL), a panel material made of a single sheet of decorative paper that’s impregnated with melamine resin and bonded to either particleboard or medium-density fiberboard (MDF).

Pro Tip: This Old House general contractor Tom Silva prefers particleboard to MDF as a substrate for laminate countertops because MDF is “too susceptible to expansion and contraction from water.” However, he makes sure to paint the underside of the particleboard to protect it from moisture damage. Is Painting Laminate Countertops a Good Idea?

Weigh the benefits of painting laminate against potential drawbacks before deciding if this project is right for you. Benefits of Painting Laminate

One of the main advantages of painting laminate countertops is the significant cost savings compared to replacement. According to Angi, a new granite countertop can cost as much as $100 per square foot, while painting is a fraction of that price. Additionally, painting allows for customization and creativity, enabling you to achieve the exact look you want. It’s also a relatively quick process that can be completed over a weekend. Potential Drawbacks To Consider

Painted laminate surfaces may not be as durable as the original laminate, and they can be more susceptible to scratches and chips. Regular maintenance and careful use are necessary to preserve the finish. Additionally, if not done correctly, the paint may peel or bubble, requiring touch-ups or even a complete redo.

Preparing Your Workspace For Laminate Countertops

Proper preparation of your workspace ensures a clean, efficient painting process and protects surrounding areas from paint and dust. Protecting Surrounding Areas

[Countertops This Old](https://countertops-this-old.northlist.shop/countertops-this-old/20260822919.html)

Before you begin, use painter’s tape to cover any areas adjacent to the countertop, such as the sink, cabinets, refrigerator, stove, and walls. Lay down drop cloths or old sheets to protect the floor and any nearby furniture. Remove any items stored on the countertop and clear the area completely. Ensuring Proper Ventilation

Painting can release fumes, so it’s crucial to work in a well-ventilated area. If possible, open windows and doors and consider using fans to circulate air. If ventilation is limited, take frequent breaks and wear a respirator mask designed for painting projects.

When taping off your workspace, opt for blue painter’s tape rather than common tan masking tape. As noted in This Old House Magazine, standard masking tape leaves an adhesive residue on surfaces, while blue painter’s tape peels off cleanly, even after several days—an important consideration for a countertop project that may take multiple coats over a weekend. Steps for Painting Laminate Countertops

Following these steps will help you achieve the best results when painting your laminate countertops. 1. Clean and Degrease the Surface

Start by thoroughly cleaning the countertop surface. Use a degreasing cleaner to remove any built-up grime, oils, or residues. This step is critical for proper paint adhesion. Rinse well and allow the surface to dry completely. 2. Sand the Countertop

Lightly sand the entire countertop surface with medium-grit sandpaper. The goal is not to sand through the laminate, but to create a slightly rough surface for the primer to adhere to. Use a sanding block or palm sander for large areas, and hand-sand corners and edges. After sanding, wipe away all dust with a tack cloth or a rag dampened with mineral spirits. Be sure to check for any areas you might’ve missed and give them a quick scuffing as well.

Also, take note of sections of laminate separating from the particle board underneath and repair them with some contact cement. 3. Apply Painter’s Tape

Once the mineral spirits have dried, apply painter’s tape along the countertop’s edges. Be sure to tape anything directly adjacent to the counter, such as the sink, cabinets, refrigerator, stove, and wall.

Apply Primer

Start by pouring some primer into the paint tray. Then, using the roller in a back-and-forth motion, apply it in thin, even coats to the laminate counter. Be sure to overlap each previous pass by a few inches to maintain a wet edge.

When you get to the inside corners and the edges, switch to a paintbrush. It’s best to brush the primer onto the counter while the rolled primer is still wet, as it will dry with a more consistent, flat texture.

Allow the primer to dry completely according to the manufacturer’s instructions before proceeding. 5. Paint the Countertop

Mix and pour some paint into the paint tray and use a fresh paint roller cover to apply it to the laminate countertop.

Be sure to overlap each stroke to ensure you’re maintaining a wet edge, just as you did with the primer. Again, switch to the brush when you reach inside corners, the sink, the backsplash, or other hard-to-reach areas.

Most laminate counters will require a second coat, so repeat the process once the first coat dries.

After the second coat, clean the paintbrush and replace the tray liner and roller cover. 6. Seal the Painted Surface

After the final coat of paint has dried completely, apply a clear topcoat sealer. This protective layer will help guard against scratches, stains, and water damage. Apply the sealer using the same techniques as the paint, ensuring even coverage. Consider applying two coats of sealer for maximum protection.

Techniques for a Professional-Looking Finish to Your Countertop

Achieving a professional-looking finish requires attention to detail and the right techniques. These professional painting tips will help you create a smooth, durable surface that looks great and lasts longer. Choosing the Right Paint

Select a high-quality, latex-based paint designed for laminate surfaces. These paints are formulated to adhere well to smooth surfaces and resist wear and tear. Consider using a paint with a slightly glossy finish, as it will be easier to clean and maintain than a matte finish. Applying Paint Evenly

Use long, smooth strokes when rolling on paint, and avoid applying too much pressure, which can create bubbles or uneven texture. Overlap each stroke slightly to blend the edges and maintain a consistent thickness. For a super-smooth finish, consider using a foam roller instead of a traditional nap roller. Creating Additional Effects

To mimic the look of stone or other materials, consider using specialty painting techniques. Sponging, rag-rolling, or using a faux finish kit can add depth and interest to your countertops. Practice these techniques on a scrap piece of laminate or cardboard before applying them to your countertops.

Maintaining Your Newly Painted Countertops

With the right care, your refreshed surfaces can keep looking great for years to come. Daily Care Tips

Use mild, nonabrasive cleaners for routine cleaning.

Wipe up spills immediately to prevent staining.

Avoid placing hot pots or pans directly on the surface.

Use cutting boards to protect the paint from knife marks. Addressing Wear and Tear

Over time, you may notice some wear on high-traffic areas. Touch up small chips or scratches with leftover paint, being careful to blend the edges. For larger areas of damage, you may need to sand, reprime, and repaint the affected section.

Alternative Methods To Refinish Laminate Countertops

While painting is a popular option, there are other methods of refreshing laminate countertops that you can consider, depending on your needs and preferences. Resurfacing Kits

Countertop resurfacing kits are available that include specially formulated coatings designed to adhere to laminate. These kits often come with everything you need for the job, including base coats, decorative chips or stone effects, and a clear topcoat. They can provide a more durable finish than paint alone. Read our guide for more detail on resurfacing from start to finish. Epoxy Coatings

Epoxy coatings offer a highly durable and glossy finish that can mimic the look of stone or other materials. While more challenging to apply than paint, epoxy can create a thick, protective layer that’s resistant to heat and scratches. However, it requires careful mixing and application to achieve the best results. Troubleshooting Common Issues

Even with careful preparation and application, you may encounter some issues when painting laminate countertops. Here’s how to address common problems. Dealing With Bubbles or Peeling

If you notice bubbles, peeling or other common paint issues after the paint has dried, it’s likely due to inadequate surface preparation or moisture. Sand down the affected area, clean thoroughly, and reapply primer and paint. Ensure the surface is completely dry before painting and that you’re working in a low-humidity environment. Fixing Uneven Color or Texture

For uneven color, apply an additional coat of paint, taking care to maintain consistent coverage. If the texture is uneven, lightly sand the entire surface and apply another coat of paint using a foam roller for a smoother finish. Our Conclusion

Painting laminate countertops is an affordable and creative way to update your kitchen or bathroom without the expense and disruption of a full renovation. With proper preparation, the right materials, and careful application, you can achieve a durable and attractive finish that breathes new life into your space.
