# Staging a House for Sale: Tips From a Pro - This Old House

Skip to content Photo by Virginia MacDonald

Who knew that getting rid of family photos and clearing off countertops could help you snag top dollar for your house? These are just two secrets from Debra Gould, founder of Staging Diva. Gould, a professional home stager, has helped scores of homeowners clean up, rearrange, and style their homes to command top price. That can mean anything from putting extra books in storage to getting rid of moldy caulk in bathrooms to renting furniture to fill up too-bare spaces.

“The goal is to make your home clean, organized, and welcoming so potential buyers can picture living there,” says Gould. Thus the no-family-photos rule: “They make people feel like they’re invading your space,” she explains.

Based in Toronto, Gould has trained a network of 800+ home stagers across the U.S. “Most owners aren’t seeing bidding wars the way they were a few years ago,” she says. “But with the right staging, you can get close to your asking price.”

Scale matters when staging, too. As stager Christopher Breining advises: “There are few things more ridiculous-looking than hanging dinky little art too high on the wall.” Keeping décor proportional to the room—whether it’s artwork, furniture, or accessories—ensures a space feels intentional rather than thrown together.

Solution: “Think hotel bathroom,” Gould says. The entire room is sparkling-clean, bottles and jars are tucked away, and the window blind is closed for privacy.

Don’t just clear the countertop—relocate surplus supplies out of the bathroom entirely. As This Old House Magazine advises, put extra soaps and shampoos in a labeled bin and move them to a closet elsewhere in the house, along with those warehouse-size packages of tissues and toilet paper. For items you do reach for daily, corral them in acrylic trays on the vanity so they look intentional rather than scattered.

Pro Tip: Nebraska-based professional organizer Amy Tokos recommends replacing towel bars with hooks, especially in family bathrooms: putting bath towels back onto bars is tough for kids’ little hands, and hooks keep wet towels from piling up on the floor. Choose individual hooks spaced far enough apart that wet towels won’t take forever to dry—a small detail that makes a bathroom look tidier for showings.
