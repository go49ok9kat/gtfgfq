Have you found a bird egg in your yard or out on your latest nature walk? If so, you’ll probably need some help identifying it—that’s where we come in. In this visual guide, you’ll find 25 common bird species and the color, markings, and size of their eggs. Plus, we’ll explain when and where you’re more likely to find these birds and their nests. Keep reading to ID your eggs and learn fun bird facts along the way! Bird Egg Identification Chart Download Image Download Image

Ornithologist Holly Garrod says it’s usually easiest to identify a bird egg by the nesting habitat you found it in (in a tree, on the ground, etc.). Eggs can also be identified by their size, markings, and color. Be mindful of the breeding season, as this will tell you when it’s more likely to find certain eggs. Steps 1 American Robin Download Article

The American Robin is common in residential areas, as well as woodlands and forests. American Robins typically lay about 1-3 broods of 3-5 eggs per season, with an incubation period of 12-14 days.[1]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source You’re more likely to find their eggs around mid-April through July, when they tend to nest and lay eggs.

Egg Color & Markings: Sky blue/blue-green with no markings

Nest Location: Trees (occasionally gutters and eaves)

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1.2 in x 0.8 in (2.8 cm x 2.1 cm) Advertisement 2 Mourning Dove Download Article

Mourning doves are common birds in open woodlands, but you’ve probably seen them in your backyard, too! They typically don’t mind nesting around humans, so you may spot them on gutters or eaves. The mourning dove lays 2 eggs per clutch with about 1-6 broods per season. Egg-laying typically starts in March and goes through September. In warmer climates, though, mourning doves may nest every month of the year.[2]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: White with no markings

Nest Location: Trees (nests on the ground are also common in the West)

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1 in x 0.8 in (2.6 cm x 2.1 cm) 3 American Goldfinch Download Article

The beautiful American Goldfinch loves weedy fields and overgrown areas, especially if there are shrubs for nesting. You’ve probably spotted them in your local neighborhood park or even in your own backyard. They typically lay 1-2 broods of 2-7 eggs per season, with a nesting season lasting from July through September.[3]XResearch source Fun fact: Goldfinches change color with the season, gaining their bright yellow plumage for the spring and summer!

Egg Color & Markings: Pale bluish white, sometimes with small brown spots around the larger end Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.6 in x 0.5 in (1.62 x 1.22 cm) Advertisement 4 Ruby-Throated Hummingbird Download Article

The Ruby-Throated Hummingbird is common in deciduous woodlands. You should be able to spot them in old fields, forest edges, and meadows, especially in eastern North America. They lay 1-3 eggs at a time and have 1-2 broods per season. They typically breed from early April to early September.[4]XResearch source Their eggs are so tiny that the mother bird uses spider silk to add extra protection to the nest!

Egg Color & Markings: Solid white with no markings

Nest Location: Trees (usually at least 10-40 feet/3.05-12.19 meters above ground)

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.5 in x 0.3 in (1.2 cm x 0.8 cm) 5 Blue Jay Download Article

Blue jays are especially common in urban and suburban areas and can often be found near oak trees. They lay only 1 brood per season with about 2-7 eggs.[5]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source Their breeding season typically begins in mid-March and goes through July. Blue jays are known for their intelligence and tight family bonds.

Egg Color & Markings: Bluish or light brown with brown spots Nest Location: Tree

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1 in x 0.7 in (2.5 cm x 1.8 cm) Advertisement 6 European Starling Download Article

Starlings live around people, so you’ve probably seen them in your yard or when you’ve been out and about. They mainly look for grassy areas and a water source and avoid large stretches of forest. Starlings lay 1-2 broods of 3-6 eggs per breeding season. They breed between mid-April and mid-July.[6]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Bluish or greenish white with no markings

Nest Location: In a cavity (like streetlights or traffic signal supports)

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1.1 in x 0.8 in (1.7 cm x 1.9 cm) 7 Northern Mockingbird Download Article

You can find the Northern Mockingbird year-round in North America. They’re common in areas with open ground, so you’re likely to spot them in parks and suburban areas. Mockingbirds love to hop around and forage on the ground, but their nests are usually higher off the ground in shrubs or trees. They lay 2-3 broods of 2-6 eggs per season. Their breeding season begins in late February and ends in late July.[7]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue or greenish white with red or brown splotches Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.8 in x 0.6 in (2 cm x 1.6 cm) Advertisement 8 Brown Thrasher Download Article

The Brown Thrasher is often found in woodlands with cottonwood, willow, or dogwood trees. Occasionally, they’ll nest in backyards or gardens, but they tend to prefer forested areas. Brown thrashers spend most of their time on or near the ground. They lay 1-2 broods of 2-6 eggs during their breeding season from mid-March to late June.[8]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Glossy pale blue or white with red-brown speckles Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1 in x 0.8 in (2.6 cm x 1.9 cm) 9 Eastern Screech Owl Download Article

Eastern Screech Owls seek out any habitat with tree coverage. They can breed in farmland, suburbs, or city parks, as long as they have some sort of cavity or nest box. They’ll even occupy abandoned woodpecker nest holes! They lay 1 brood per season with 2-6 eggs.[9]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to sourceEastern Screech Owls typically lay their eggs in mid-March and April.

Egg Color & Markings: White with no markings Nest Location: Holes and cavities

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1.3 in x 1.1 in (3.4 cm x 2.9 cm) Advertisement 10 American Barn Owl Download Article

American Barn Owls live in open areas like grasslands, deserts, suburbs, and cities, as long as there are structures to build their nests in. They make their nests out of their own regurgitated pellets and typically use their nest throughout the year. The American Barn Owl lays 1-3 broods of 2-18 eggs per season. Their breeding season is typically between March and July.[10]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Dull white, often with dirt from the nest

Nest Location: Manmade structures like barn lofts, church steeples, and houses

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1.5 in x 1.2 in (3.9 cm x 3.1 cm) 11 Great Blue Heron Download Article

You can find Great Blue Herons in freshwater or saltwater habitats. If you live near a lake, marsh, or pond, you’ll probably be able to spot one of these gorgeous birds! Great Blue Herons nest in colonies that can consist of 500 or more individual nests. They lay 1-2 broods of 2-6 eggs per breeding season. They typically lay their eggs between April and June.[11]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue, often fading with age Nest Location: Trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 2.4 in x 1.8 in (6.1 cm x 4.5 cm) Advertisement 12 Peregrine Falcon Download Article

Peregrine Falcons can be found all over the world with nests as high up as 12,000 feet (3,657.6 meters)! They typically nest on cliffsides, but you might also spot them on electrical towers or skyscrapers. Peregrine Falcons are strong fliers and often reported as the fastest bird in the world. They typically lay 1 brood of 2-5 eggs in late April and early May.[12]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source

Egg Color & Markings: Creamy to brownish with brown, red, or purple blotches Nest Location: Cliffsides

Egg Size (Length x Width): 2 in x 1.6 in (5 cm x 4 cm) 13 Bald Eagle Download Article

Bald Eagles usually nest in forested areas near a large body of water. They tend to avoid developed areas, so you’re less likely to see them in your yard or your local park. Bald Eagles lay 1 brood of 1-3 eggs per season (usually in January or February).[13]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source

Egg Color & Markings: Dull white with no markings Nest Location: Trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 2.3 in x 1.9 in (5.8 cm x 4.7 cm) Advertisement 14 Northern Cardinal Download Article

[Bird Egg](https://bird-egg.northlist.xyz/bird-egg/20260826.html)

Northern Cardinals love dense shrubby areas. You can find them in overgrown fields, backyards, or forest edges. They tend to hop through low branches and forage on the ground, so they’re usually pretty easy to spot. Northern Cardinals lay 1-2 broods of 2-5 eggs per season. They lay their eggs between March and August.[14]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Grayish or greenish white with pale gray or brown speckles Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.9 in x 0.7 in (2.2 cm x 1.7 cm) 15 House Sparrow Download Article

As their name implies, House Sparrows are common in residential areas. You can look for them outside your home, or in cities, towns, suburbs, and farms. House sparrows avoid extreme environments and can only survive in the immediate vicinity of people.[15]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source They lay 1-4 broods of 1-8 eggs per season, with a breeding season from April to August.

Egg Color & Markings: White or greenish/bluish-white with gray or brown spots Nest Location: Cavities

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.8 in x 0.6 in (2 cm x 1.4 cm) Advertisement 16 Red-Winged Blackbird Download Article

Red-Winged Blackbirds like to nest near fresh or saltwater marshes. You can occasionally find them in wooded areas along waterways. While they nest near the ground, male Red-Winged Blackbirds can be found singing on high perches during their breeding season. Red-Winged Blackbirds lay 1-2 broods of 2-4 eggs per season. They tend to lay their eggs between May and June.[16]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue-green to gray with black or brown markings Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.9 in x 0.6 in (2.2 cm x 1.6 cm) 17 American Crow Download Article

American Crows are super adaptable and can live anywhere with trees to perch in and a source of food. You can see them in natural or manmade habitats such as farms, city parks, pastures, or vacant lots. They’re very social birds and can usually be seen in groups. They lay 1-2 broods of 3-9 eggs per season, with nest building occurring in late April and May.[17]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue-green to olive green with brown and gray blotches near the larger end Nest Location: Trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 1.4 in x 1 in (3.6 cm x 2.6 cm) Advertisement 18 Downy Woodpecker Download Article

The Downy Woodpecker loves open woodlands, especially along streams. However, you can also find them in orchards, parks, and suburbs. They tend to nest in dead trees, creating cavities inside small stubs. Downy Woodpeckers typically stick to the trees, but you can occasionally see them hopping on the ground for food. They lay 1 brood of eggs with 3-8 eggs per season. They lay their eggs between May and June.[18]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Completely white with no markings Nest Location: Cavities in trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.8 in x 0.6 in (1.9 cm x 1.4 cm) 19 Black-Capped Chickadee Download Article

You can find Chickadees in forests, open woods, willow thickets, and cottonwood groves. They often excavate their own nesting cavities but will also use nest boxes, natural cavities, or abandoned Downy Woodpecker cavities. They are highly social birds and live in flocks. Chickadees lay 1 brood of 1-13 eggs during their May to June season.[19]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: White with reddish-brown spots Nest Location: Cavities

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.6 in x 0.5 in (1.5 cm x 1.2 cm) Advertisement 20 Great Tit Download Article

Great Tits can be found across Europe in open woodlands, gardens, city parks, and orchards. They use nest boxes, natural tree cavities, or holes in buildings to build their nests. They’re active and vocal birds and will often visit bird feeders. Great Tits lay 1-3 broods of 3-18 eggs during their season between April and May.[20]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: White with reddish-brown speckles Nest Location: Cavities

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.7 in x 0.55 in (1.8 cm x 1.4 cm) 21 Osprey Download Article

Ospreys live along shorelines, where they can find shallow fishing grounds—anywhere they can find a good supply of fish. They prefer to nest high up on tree tops or on cliffsides to stay safe from ground predators. Ospreys lay 1 brood of 1-4 eggs during their season and often roost alone or in small flocks. They tend to lay their eggs by the end of April.[21]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Cream to pinkish with a wreath of reddish brown spots Nest Location: Trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 2.2 in x 1.6 in (5.5 cm x 4.2 cm) Advertisement 22 Northern Yellow Warbler Download Article

Northern Yellow Warblers can be found in thickets and other regrowing habitats along streams and wetlands. They’ll typically build their nest in the vertical forks of shrubs or small trees, opting for elevated locations closer to the ground. Northern Yellow Warblers lay 1-2 broods of 1-7 eggs per season.[22]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source Their egg-laying season occurs around late May to mid-June.

Egg Color & Markings: Grayish or greenish white with dark spots Nest Location: Shrubs

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.6 in x 0.5 in (1.5 cm x 1.2 cm) 23 Tufted Titmouse Download Article

Tufted Titmice are common in deciduous woods, orchards, parks, and suburban areas. They nest in naturally occurring cavities since they’re unable to dig their own—they tend to reuse old nest holes made by woodpeckers. They lay 1 brood of 3-9 eggs during their breeding season, which occurs between April and mid-July.[23]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: White to cream with red, brown, purple, or lilac spots Nest Location: Cavities

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.7 in x 0.6 in (1.7 cm x 1.4 cm) Advertisement 24 House Finch Download Article

House Finches are a common bird found in manmade habitats such as buildings, lawns, and urban centers. While they mainly nest in trees, you can also find them nesting in vents, ledges, street lamps, and hanging planters. They’re very social and can form flocks as large as several hundred birds. They lay 1-6 broods of 2-6 eggs during their season from March to August.[24]XTrustworthy SourceThe Cornell Lab of OrnithologyResearch lab of Cornell University dedicated to interpreting and conserving the Earth's biological diversity through research and education about birdsGo to source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue to white with small black and purple speckles Nest Location: Trees

Egg Size (Length x Width): 0.6 in x 0.5 in (1.6 cm x 1.3 cm) 25 Cedar Waxwing Download Article

Cedar Waxwings mainly live in woodlands along streams, but they are becoming increasingly common in towns and suburbs. You can typically spot their nests in maple, pine, red cedar, apple, or pear trees—they like to build their nests on forks of horizontal branches. Cedar Waxwings lay 1-2 broods of 2-6 eggs during their season from early June through mid-August.[25]XResearch source

Egg Color & Markings: Pale blue or blue-gray with black or gray spotting Nest Location: Trees
