What to Consider When Choosing a French-Door Refrigerator

French-door fridges mainly differ in capacity, configuration, energy-efficiency level, and the finish in which they’re available. Additionally, some models may offer smart technology or be more aesthetically pleasing than others. Before getting too deep into a search for the best French-door refrigerator, shoppers may want to make sure they carefully measure the kitchen space it will occupy and determine priorities and preferences. Doing so helps narrow down the model that will best meet specific individual requirements. Finish

Finish is one of the first things that shoppers notice about traditional or French-door fridges, and it will greatly affect how the appliance looks in a space. Some common material and color finishes for French-door refrigerators include: Stainless steel Black stainless steel Matte black stainless steel White Black Slate Size and Capacity

The size and capacity of a French-door refrigerator are also very important to consider. Capacity refers to how much interior space the average refrigerator and freezer offer, which affects the amount of food that can be stored inside.

Capacity is measured in cubic feet, and most French-door refrigerators tend to offer capacities ranging between 21 and 30 cubic feet. To better conceptualize capacity, 1 cubic foot generally equals one bag of groceries. A 19- to 22-cubic-foot refrigerator is usually sufficient for a family of four with average shopping habits.

In addition to considering how much fridge capacity is thought to be needed, it’s also important to measure a refrigerator’s overall size and dimensions to confirm that it will fit in the space available. Shoppers looking to buy a new French-door refrigerator will want to measure the space in the kitchen carefully and check those dimensions against the length, height, and depth of each French-door refrigerator being considered. Those who have limited space in their kitchen or prefer an appliance that’s flush with their countertops may want to consider a counter-depth refrigerator. Configuration

A French-door refrigerator’s configuration affects how functional it is and how well it is able to meet individual demands. When browsing fridge configurations, shoppers may want to consider the overall layout of the shelves, the size and arrangement of the storage bins in the doors, and the design and location of the produce, meat, and other temperature-controlled drawers.

Because they can accommodate containers of various sizes, refrigerators with adjustable shelves are the most versatile and are preferred by many households. In addition to adjustable shelves, some French-door refrigerators may also have shelves that partially slide back to fit tall items, such as stockpots.

It’s also important to think about the way in which the fridge’s freezer is designed. Though all French-door refrigerators feature a pullout drawer freezer, the insides of the freezers may be laid out differently. If there is an ice maker, it should always be easy for users to access.

Freezers that have stacked shelves inside that slide in and out separately make it easier to access and organize the freezer’s contents. Some French-door refrigerators may even have two separate drawers: one freezer drawer and a smaller drawer with an adjustable temperature, which enables households to apportion that additional space to beverages, frozen foods, vegetables, or other frequently used items. Energy Rating

A French-door refrigerator’s energy rating discloses the approximate annual electricity costs of operating the refrigerator. Every appliance should be accompanied by an Energy Guide sticker or pamphlet that shows the cost range for operating similar French-door refrigerators. Before buying, shoppers may want to compare the Energy Guide data of several models to find the most energy-efficient appliance that will save money on household electric bills each year.

French-door refrigerators with an Energy Star label have met rigorous energy-efficiency standards that reduce energy costs and a household’s environmental footprint. Energy Star refrigerators typically use about 33 percent less energy than older, less efficient models. Smart Technology

Smart French-door refrigerators, also called connected refrigerators, offer more advanced features than are found on a standard refrigerator. Smart refrigerators can connect with other smart devices in the home to allow users to adjust the appliance’s temperature through their smartphone, tablet, or personal home assistant.

Other features that can be found in a smart refrigerator include autofill sensors for filling cups, the ability to create and share shopping lists or memos, and advanced climate-control settings. A few smart refrigerators are designed to allow users to tap on the door to see inside the refrigerator, which allows owners to browse for food without opening the door and letting cold air out. Aesthetics

Owners will have to look at their new French-door refrigerator multiple times a day, so it’s important not to discount the aesthetics of the models being considered. One detail that can affect a refrigerator’s appeal is the finish color, as mentioned above. Choosing a finish color that coordinates or matches with other appliances, countertops, and cabinets can go a long way in creating a cohesive-looking kitchen.

Many people prefer the look of a built-in refrigerator because it blends in better with the cabinets and makes the refrigerator look like it truly belongs in the space. Additional Features

Once factors like the refrigerator’s finish, capacity, and energy rating are decided, it’s time to evaluate extra features that make French-door refrigerators stand out. Some add-ons shoppers may want to consider include:

Door-within-door: Some French-door refrigerators have a small panel on one of the doors that can be opened separately to provide quick access to a few items stored in the door’s bins. Opening just the panel rather than the whole door reduces how much cold air escapes from the refrigerator and, in turn, keeps energy costs down.

Temperature-controlled drawer: Several French-door refrigerators provide independent temperature or humidity control for drawers, providing the perfect environment for fruit, vegetables, or meat. It may also be called a deli drawer.

Ice and water dispensers: Many French-door refrigerators have Ice and water dispensers for convenient access to these necessities. Some water dispensers are on the exterior of the refrigerator, while others may be inside.

Water filters: French-door refrigerators may have built-in water filters to remove impurities for cleaner and better-tasting drinking water.

Programmable controls: French-door refrigerators with programmable controls allow users to adjust the refrigerator’s temperature, lock the water or ice dispensers, check on the water filter’s status, and set a predetermined amount of water to be dispensed each time the water fill button is pressed.

Fingerprint-resistant finishes: Refrigerators that are fingerprint-resistant feature a unique finish that reduces smudges and fingerprints and keeps the surface looking tidy. FAQs

Q. What is the life expectancy of a French-door refrigerator?

A French-door refrigerator typically has a life expectancy of 10 to 15 years, according to most manufacturers and leading retailers, although 20 years is not unusual. French-door refrigerators tend to last longer when cared for properly, and where practical, they should be rolled away from the wall a couple of times a year so the area behind them can be cleaned. Leading brands like those in our top picks may also outlast imported budget brands.

Q. What is the point of a 4-door refrigerator?

Some people find that having extra doors makes a four-door refrigerator easier to organize and view its content. With drawers, items often get stacked on top of each other, and so some of them are inevitably hidden. Of course, this depends on how you like to organize your refrigerator. Confusingly, the four-door name is often applied to French-door refrigerators with two doors and two drawers. In this case, buyers might get the benefit of a temperature-controlled drawer or deli drawer. The important thing is not how the model is described but how well the layout and features fit a household’s needs.

Q. What size refrigerator do I need for a family of four?

In most cases, a 19- to 22-cubic-foot refrigerator should be sufficient for a family of four. However, families who prefer to shop in bulk or stock up on their supermarket trips may find that a larger refrigerator, such as one with a capacity closer to 30 cubic feet, better suits their needs.

Q. Do French-door refrigerators have ice maker problems?

It would be unfair to suggest that all French-door refrigerators have ice maker problems. But, historically, the ice dispenser has been a weak point in some models, and although they are not common, some faults do still occur.
