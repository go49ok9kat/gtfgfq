For instance, installing a light fixture takes less time, effort, and material than rewiring an entire room or house. Common Issues an Electrician Can Fix

An electrician can tackle any major or minor electrical issue within a building. Common home electrical issues can include:

Flickering lights: A flickering light typically means there is an electrical grid issue that is hindering the transfer of electricity into that system. If a light still flickers after the bulb has been changed, there may be a faulty fixture or fraying wires, which can start a fire if left unaddressed.

Tripped circuit breaker: Typically a circuit breaker shuts down or is “tripped” when it is overloaded with electricity. This is a built-in safety measure to prevent overheating that can cause electrical fires. If unplugging the device and resetting the circuit doesn’t work, an electrician should be able to diagnose the issue.

Rising electricity bill: Home electricity bills are typically higher in the summer when the air conditioning is running for most of the season. However, if there is no discernable difference in your electricity use and the bill is increasing month after month, an electrician can help assess the home’s electricity usage and make sure there are no mechanical issues within the systems.

Exposed wires: Any exposed wiring in a building can pose a serious safety hazard, and it’s best to call an electrician as soon as the problem is spotted. Aside from being a fire risk, bare wires can cause painful and severe electrical shocks if a person or animal comes into contact with them. An electrician can secure any open wiring and hide it safely behind a switch or outlet.

Loose electrical contacts: A plug that repeatedly falls out of an outlet isn’t just annoying—it’s a sign of a loose contact within a socket, which can ultimately cause electrical arcing that might lead to house fires. Electricians can inspect the outlet and tighten it up to ensure a secure connection.

An electrician typically doesn’t fix television or internet issues (cable and internet service providers can troubleshoot any of those problems). Additionally, if there’s a power outage that impacts an entire block or town, the local utility company will be called in to resolve it, rather than an individual electrician. Certain home emergencies will require a professional electrician’s expertise to avoid a potential fire hazard or safety issue, including:

Loss of power: Storms, downed electrical lines, and other accidents may cut off power to a home. If your home has lost power and your local utility company hasn’t reported a wider outage, you may want to call an electrician to determine why.

Buzzing: A loud buzzing or humming sound near the breaker box could mean that a faulty breaker is failing to trip due to poor wiring. This can be a hazardous situation leading to electrical failures and house fires, so an electrician should be called as soon as possible.

Burned outlets: Black or brown marks around an electrical outlet means they’re likely overloading and getting too hot, which leads to a risk of a fire. An electrician near you can assess the issue, replace the outlet, and rewire the system if necessary.
