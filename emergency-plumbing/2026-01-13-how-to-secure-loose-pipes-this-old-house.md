If your blocking piece is too long to fit between the studs, cut each of the corners off at an angle. This allows you to maneuver it up inside the wall cavity and then tip it into position. Pre-drill the blocking before toenailing it to the studs so the wood doesn’t split.

Pro Tip: When dealing with copper pipes that expand and create noise, a This Old House plumber explained that mechanical expansion joints—which move back and forth to absorb expansion—are one option, but cautioned: “We don’t use these often anymore because inside here there’s a gasket that can dry up. If it does, it could leak into the floor.” Simpler fixes like pipe straps and cushioned hangers are usually all you need for loose pipes.

Richard, This Old House’s plumbing expert, explains the urgency of fixing a loose shower valve: “If this valve is moving this much, those pipes are moving and that’s not good.” Loose pipes behind the wall can lead to water getting inside the wall cavity, causing hidden damage over time.
