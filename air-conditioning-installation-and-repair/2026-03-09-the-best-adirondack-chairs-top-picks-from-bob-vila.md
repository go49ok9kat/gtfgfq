What to Consider When Choosing an Adirondack Chair

Adirondack chairs represent a major investment in backyard seating. Whether shoppers prefer traditional weather-resistant woods or are more inclined toward the plastic variety, the top Adirondack chairs bring style and comfort to an outdoor area. To help consumers make sure they’re getting the best of the best, the following guide outlines key features and considerations that often factor into the decision. Material

The best outdoor Adirondack chairs are typically made of wood. Cedar, teak, redwood, and Shorea (a tropical hardwood similar to teak) naturally resist insects, sun, and weather damage, while eucalyptus and acacia are also considered top bug- and weather-resistant materials.

Oak and pine are common, too, but both bring distinct advantages and disadvantages. Although oak is strong and durable, it doesn’t resist sun damage as well as other woods. Neither does pine, which doesn’t hold up well in poor weather. So though pine may be more affordable, it’s a less suitable wooden chair option for those looking for longevity.

Plastic is another option. Though this type typically varies in quality, the best plastic Adirondack chairs are usually made of top-quality resins. These chairs cost about the same as the wood variety but imitate them well in appearance and feel, with some weighing almost as much as their all-natural counterparts. Durability

The best all-weather Adirondack chairs have sturdy construction, quality material, and protective finishes. Wood varieties like cedar, acacia, eucalyptus, and redwoods offer the best weather and bug resistance but need a new coat of stain or treatment with a wood sealer at least once a year to maintain their aesthetic appeal. Keeping a protective cover over wooden chairs when they aren’t in use helps preserve them, too.

Durable Adirondack chairs made of high-quality plastics resist water, don’t attract bugs, and are available in various color choices that consumers won’t find with wood varieties. Chairs made with UV- and weather-treated plastic rival wood in durability and often surpass it in portability, since plastic weighs less than wood.

When properly maintained, plastic and plastic-composite Adirondack chairs typically last for 10 years or longer. Placing a protective cover over them during harsh weather to prevent fading and wear and tear can extend their lifespan. Portability

A full-size Adirondack chair isn’t exactly easy to carry. Long and sometimes heavy, some solid wood models weigh more than 100 pounds. If shoppers are looking for Adirondack chairs for the beach or the lake, they may want to consider buying small, folding versions of these chairs.

Folding Adirondack chairs collapse in on themselves for easier storage and carrying. These make the best Adirondack chairs for fire pit gatherings, tailgating sessions, camping trips, and other outdoor events. Plastic options tend to weigh less than wood varieties, so a folding Adirondack chair made of plastic or plastic-composite materials may be the best bet in terms of portability. Style

Adirondack chairs come in a variety of colors, from natural, stained-wood styles to colorful, patterned options. Traditional wooden Adirondack chairs feature an angled back with a rounded top and large armrests. This classic and simple look fits in with most decor types and traditional homes in particular. Deep wooden hues often work best in this case.

For those who want to buck tradition, a wide array of other styles offer whimsical deviations from the standard. For modern homes, consider a contemporary Adirondack chair that features straight rather than curved lines. Lighter colors make a great pick for coastal or beachside outdoor seating. Bright colors and patterns suit the eclectic urban porch. Additional Features

Q. Can you leave Adirondack chairs outside?

That depends on the material your chairs are made of and what the weather’s like where you live. Some woods and plastics can withstand harsh weather elements year-round. Others can’t. For example, HDPE can be left outside in any weather since it is resistant to water, rust, and wind.

Q. What color Adirondack chair should I get?

Color choice depends on your style and how you want to use them. If you want a natural look, cedar, teak, or acacia can do the trick. But plastic Adirondacks offer more color varieties if you are interested in a pop of color in your backyard or cottage space.

Q. What are the most popular colors for Adirondack chairs?

Wood tones are among the most popular colors for Adirondack chairs since they offer a classic look for a backyard or outdoor space. But many also prefer navy blue, gray, and white (usually found with plastic models) since these are neutral colors that can accommodate most outdoor areas.

Q. How do you clean an Adirondack chair?

Wooden Adirondack chairs can be cleaned with a power washer and then wiped down with a damp cloth to protect the wood from damage. For plastic and resin, use water, mild soap, and a cloth or sponge to remove debris and dirt. Just remember to let the chair dry completely before storing to ensure that mold and mildew do not build up.

Q. How do you store an Adirondack chair?

While most Adirondack chairs include weather treatments, storing them under a cover and in a shed helps them last longer. Folding chairs are the easiest to store in small spaces like garages or sheds. If you prefer the cover route of storing your chairs, ensure that the cover you choose is made with premium vinyl to maximize protection from the elements. Q. How long do Adirondack chairs last?

The typical lifespan of an Adirondack chair is about 10 years. It can last longer than that with proper care, including storing the chair(s) during winter months, cleaning regularly, and preventing damage.

Q. How do you paint an Adirondack chair?

First, clean the chair, removing dirt and particles. Once dry, use a brush to apply oil-based paint to a wooden chair or standard exterior gloss paint to a plastic one. For ample durability, wooden Adirondack chairs can also be painted with exterior latex enamel paint for increased longevity.
