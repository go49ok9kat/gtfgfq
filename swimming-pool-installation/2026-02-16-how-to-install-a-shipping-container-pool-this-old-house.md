Potential Drawbacks of Shipping Container Pools

While shipping container pools have many benefits, they may not work for everyone. Here are some things to consider:

Depth limitations: These pools are typically around 5 1/2 feet deep.

Limited shape options: Most are rectangular, which may not suit all yard layouts.

Permitting: Some areas may have regulations against these pool types.

How Shipping Container Pools Are Manufactured

Creating a shipping container pool is a fascinating process that transforms an ordinary steel box into a stylish swimming pool. Here’s how it works. Preparation and Cutting

[Install Shipping Container](https://install-shipping-container.northlist.shop/install-shipping-container/20260822707.html)

The process begins with the delivery of shipping containers to the manufacturing facility. Workers measure and mark the pool’s depth. Using a grinder, they cut along these marks and remove the top portion of the container. Adding Features

Once the top is removed, the real transformation begins. Welders add different features to the container’s inside, including the following: Benches Steps Thick, steel rim (as a wall cap) Window frames (for underwater viewing) Finishing Touches

The raw steel interior is then sandblasted clean and coated with primer. Finally, a multilayer topcoat is applied, which protects the pool and gives it color. This coating is highly elastic, allowing it to expand and contract with temperature changes without cracking. The manufacturers then set up the mechanical closet beneath the steps, which houses all of the pool’s equipment in a hidden space.

Before being sent to its destination, the pool undergoes a rigorous testing period, which includes filling it with water to test the coating, lines, and jets, before being emptied and dried for shipping. Site Preparation for Pool Installation

Before the pool is delivered, take time to thoroughly prepare the site where you plan to install it with these steps:

Excavation: Level an area large enough for the pool, plus an additional 4 feet for equipment access.

Reinforcement: Install helical piers at each corner to support the pool’s weight.

Concrete work: Pour concrete footers over the piers.

Retaining wall: If necessary, build a retaining wall to support the surrounding earth.

Utility trenches: Dig trenches for electrical and gas lines from the house to the pool site.

Drainage: Add drainage gravel to the base and install a dry well for potential water removal. The Installation Process

With site preparation complete, it’s time for the exciting part—installing the pool. Here’s how it typically unfolds:

Delivery: The pool is transported to the site on a flatbed truck.

Crane lift: A large crane lifts the pool over any obstacles and lowers it onto the prepared foundation.

Positioning: Workers carefully adjust the pool’s position to make sure it’s perfectly level on the footings.

Utility connections: Electricians and plumbers connect the pool to power and gas lines.

Shipping Container Pool Maintenance and Care

While shipping container pools are generally low-maintenance, they still require some care. Don’t forget about the following:

Chemical balance: Test and adjust water chemistry weekly.

Filter maintenance: Clean or replace the cartridge filter as recommended by the manufacturer.

Regular cleaning: Skim the surface and vacuum the pool as needed.

Winter preparation: In colder climates, properly winterize the pool to prevent damage. Pool Cost Considerations

The cost of a shipping container pool varies by size, features, and installation complexity. According to the video, prices range from $16,000–$40,000.
