The result is a second floor that packs a hall bath with the home’s only bathtub, a guest bedroom enlivened by an unexpected mix of soft paint colors, and the elevated loft hangout—all without feeling cramped.

The basement office is part of a broader strategy in the Idea House to tap underutilized areas for hardworking spaces. As This Old House Magazine noted, “You don’t have to add space to create a home office; you can also tap underutilized areas. In our Idea House, a basement brightened by a landscaped egress window well makes a cheery home office, and a tricked-out garage illuminated by skylights becomes a versatile maker space.”

To succeed, a basement office needs light and personality. In ours, unique plantable window wells provide light and a pretty view, while Enright added the personality. She started with a floor of click-together linoleum tile laid in 24-inch-wide black and bone stripes to give the space a “fun, unexpected, kind of retro look.”

Then she added bold, whimsical photo prints of vintage bathing costumes. Long curtains visually raise the ceiling and help the space feel finished, as do a sunny accent wall and airy furniture. The result: a space that’s energizing, not oppressive.
