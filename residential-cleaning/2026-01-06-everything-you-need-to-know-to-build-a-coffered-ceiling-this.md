Prepping for Your Coffered Ceiling Build

If you follow our plan, your ceiling project will take two days. On the first day, you’ll install the beams (steps 1-11 below). On the second day, you’ll install molding and paint (steps 12-15).

Pro Tip: Before you begin, gather your tools and materials. You’ll need a stud finder, chalk line with nonstaining chalk, miter saw, drill/driver with an 18-inch bit, pneumatic nailers (16-gauge and 18-gauge), rafter square, putty knife, caulk gun, a 2½-inch sash brush, and a small paint roller. For materials, pick up six 16-foot 1×6 primed finger-jointed pine boards, cove-profile shoe molding, panel adhesive, 2-inch 16-gauge pneumatic nails, 1-inch 18-gauge pneumatic brads, wood shims, wood glue, wood putty, latex caulk, and semigloss paint. Creating a Coffered Ceiling: Step-by-Step

While it may seem like a daunting project, even DIY enthusiasts can create a stunning coffered ceiling with the right approach. This Old House contributor Chris Beidel demonstrates how to construct a low-profile coffered ceiling that simplifies the process of working overhead.

Pro Tip: This Old House general contractor Tom Silva says the trick is to build U-shaped beams on the floor to minimize overhead work. Use a stud finder to locate the ceiling joists. Mark both ends of each joist where it meets the sidewalls. Measure for the first main perimeter beam. If your room is square or irregular, start with a perimeter beam that runs perpendicular to the joists to create a firm connection to the ceiling (if you need two boards to span the length of your room, join them with a plain scarf joint at a joist).

Cut the beam on your miter saw ¼ inch too long. Dry-fit the piece, then apply panel adhesive to the length of the beam. Butt one end into the corner and hold it there with your deadman, as shown.

Tip: The beams need to be tight to the ceiling at the joists, but any gaps that appear between joists will be covered later by the molding. Step 3: Nail the Beam in Place Ryan Benyi

Tuck the other end into the opposite corner, creating a bow. Begin at one end and work across, firing two 16-gauge pneumatic nails into each joist with your pneumatic nailer. Voids will be covered with molding and caulk. Measure and cut the opposite perimeter beam, and repeat the installation process.

Despite careful planning and execution, you may encounter some issues during installation. Here are a few common ones. Gaps Between Beams and Ceiling

If you notice gaps between the beams and the ceiling, use moldings to cover these imperfections. Carefully measure and cut to ensure a snug fit that hides any discrepancies. Difficulty in Aligning Beams

Aligning beams perfectly can be tricky. Use a laser level to ensure accuracy. Nail Holes and Imperfections
