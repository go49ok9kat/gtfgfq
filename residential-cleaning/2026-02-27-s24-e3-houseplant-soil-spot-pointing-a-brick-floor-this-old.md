For succulents, the soil should feel sandy to the touch. As Jenn demonstrated on Ask This Old House, succulent soil will “capture the water, but it’ll also run right through so it’s not in standing water.” The mix also includes perlite and coconut coir, which add different textures for roots to grab onto. When masons point brick, they need to wet the brick to make sure it doesn’t pull too much of the moisture out of the mortar. When that doesn’t happen, the mortar becomes crumbly and dry. When a homeowner’s breezeway brick floor mortar started to crumble, he called the team at Ask This Old House for help. Mason Mark McCullough knew just how to solve it. Where to find it? Pro Tip: Mason Mark McCullough explains the root cause of crumbling mortar: “If you skip that step, the moisture gets sucked out of the mortar as it dries and you’re left with what we call dusty mortar.” He reassured homeowner Joe that despite the damage, the fix was straightforward: “I do see all the problems, and the good news is, Joe, that we can fix them all. We’re going to use a method that we call spot pointing.”

When removing old mortar, Mark McCullough advises going at least three-quarters of an inch deep—the standard for repointing. “If you can get down an inch, I like it more because the mortar just becomes stronger that we put in,” he says. He also cautions to keep your chisel away from the edge of the brick: “You sneak it in, hammer.” If a damaged joint is too large for a simple spot point, Mark recommends extending the repair at least the width of the brick to ensure a solid bond.
