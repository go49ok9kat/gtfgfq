Your old wooden furniture may have collected a layer of grime that makes it difficult for you to see the beautiful finish that it once had. But have no fear! With proper cleaning and maintenance, your old wooden furniture will be restored back to its original shine in no time. We'll show you how to wipe away that layer of grime, then scrub off the stains. Then, we'll fill you in on some easy maintenance to make sure your old furniture looks as spiffy as the day it was made.

The Best Way to Clean Old Wood Furniture

Antiques expert Reyne Hirsch recommends dusting the wood furniture with a soft towel to start. Then, mix a few drops of mild dishwashing soap with warm water. Dampen a soft cloth with the soapy water and gently wipe down the wood. Then, dry the furniture with a dry cloth. Steps Method 1 Method 1 of 3: Using a Mild Cleaning Solution Download Article 1

Test dishwashing soap on an inconspicuous location. Before you clean your old wooden furniture with dishwashing soap, you need to test it out to make sure it won't damage the wood or the finish.[1]XExpert SourceGuy Peters

House Cleaning ProfessionalExpert Interview Take a damp cotton ball, add 1 drop of liquid dish soap, then wipe it on a hidden location, like the inside of a chair leg. If the detergent strips or ruins the finish, do not use it.[2]XResearch source

Wait about 5 minutes after you apply the soap before you check it to make sure it won't damage your old wooden furniture.

[Removal and Care](https://removal-and-care.swapstreet.shop/removal-and-care/20260826.html)

If the soap damages the finish, clean it with just water. 2

Mix mild dishwashing soap and warm water to form a cleaning solution. In a medium-sized bucket, combine 2 tablespoons (30 mL) of dishwashing soap and 1⁄2 gallon (1.9 L) of warm water.[3]XExpert SourceGuy Peters

House Cleaning ProfessionalExpert Interview Stir them well to combine them. Make sure the soap is fully combined with the water and you have a frothy cleaning solution.[4]XResearch source Advertisement 3

Wipe the wooden furniture clean with the soap and water. Dip a soft cloth in the solution and wring it out to remove excess water. Wipe down the entire piece of furniture, making sure you get into the nooks and crannies. Rub the cloth lightly over the surface of the wood in a circular motion.[5]XExpert SourceGuy Peters

House Cleaning ProfessionalExpert Interview

Rinse the cloth whenever it's visibly dirty. Be sure to wring it out thoroughly whenever you soak it in the cleaning solution.

Do not soak or saturate the wood or you could damage it! 4

Use non-gel toothpaste and a toothbrush to clean the crevices. If your old wooden furniture has stains in nooks and crannies that are hard to reach, apply some non-gel toothpaste to the area and wait 5 minutes to let it soak in. Then, take a soft-bristled toothbrush and gently scrub the toothpaste away.[6]XResearch source

Tip: Brush in a soft, circular motion to lift the stains. 5

Dry the wood completely with a dry cloth. When you've finished wiping down the furniture with the cleaning solution, take a fresh, clean cloth and go over the surface of the wood to dry and buff it. Make sure every bit of the furniture is completely dry.

Use a lint-free cloth to avoid leaving any residue on the furniture. Advertisement Method 2 Method 2 of 3: Removing Stains Download Article 1

Use tea to restore the shine of the old wood. Boil 1 US quart (0.95 L) of water in a pot and steep 2 black tea bags in it for 10 minutes or until the water cools to room temperature. Take a clean, soft cloth, dip it into the tea, and wring out the excess water. Lightly wipe down the entire surface of the wood, but don't saturate the wood with the tea.[7]XResearch source

The tannic acid in the tea will help maintain the wood and restore its shine. 2

Mix baking soda and non-gel toothpaste to remove water stains. To remove difficult water rings from your old wooden furniture, combine equal parts baking soda and non-gel toothpaste, and apply it directly to the stain. Rub the mixture with a soft cloth until the stain is removed.[8]XResearch source

[Wooden Furniture](https://wooden-furniture.themaplelane.com/wooden-furniture/20260826.html)

Make sure you dry the wood thoroughly after you remove the baking soda and toothpaste mixture. 3

Remove difficult marks with baking soda and water. For especially stubborn marks, like ink or scuff marks, mix together 1 tablespoon (15 mL) of baking soda and 1 teaspoon (4.9 mL) of water to form a paste. Apply the paste directly to the stain and gently rub it with a clean, soft cloth until the stain is removed.[9]XResearch source

Wipe the paste completely off of the wood and make sure the wood is thoroughly dry. 4

Wipe a layer of lemon oil on the wood to preserve the finish. After you've cleaned your old wooden furniture, wipe a layer of commercially prepared lemon oil over the entire surface to preserve the finish and make it shine. Apply the lemon oil in a circular motion for an even coat.[10]XResearch source

Tip: Make your own polishing mixture by combining 1 cup (240 mL) of olive oil and 1⁄4 cup (59 mL) cup of white vinegar. Advertisement Method 3 Method 3 of 3: Maintaining Old Wooden Furniture Download Article 1

Dust your old wooden furniture regularly. An easy way to prevent the buildup of dust that can lead to grime and stains is to dust the furniture at least once every 3 months. Use a duster or a clean cloth to wipe down any dust that's collected on the furniture.[11]XExpert SourceGuy Peters

House Cleaning ProfessionalExpert Interview

Don't use spray dusters like Pledge on old wooden furniture or it could damage the wood or the finish. 2

Keep your furniture out of direct sunlight. Place your old wooden furniture away from windows or anywhere else where the UV light from the sun can reach it. The sunlight can warp and damage the wood.[12]XResearch source

Don't keep your old wooden furniture outside or it will start to break down. 3

Check for pests or insects in the furniture. Mice, rats, roaches, and termites can damage the integrity of your furniture. The soft wood of the old furniture can make it an especially attractive meal to rodents and pests who will chew through the wood.[13]XResearch source

If your furniture is infested, call an exterminator immediately.

Tip: Check for crumbles or bite marks in the wood to see if a pest is eating it. 4

Store your old wooden furniture in a dark, dry place. Heat and humidity can damage the old wood of your furniture so store it in a dry place that isn't hotter than 85 °F (29 °C). Place a protective cover over the furniture to keep it from being scuffed or damaged.

Do not store your old wooden furniture in an attic unless it is climate controlled. Advertisement Join the Discussion...
